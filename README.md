# Sprint-Review-and-Retrospective
Final Project for CS-250 detailing Scrum and Agile Practices

How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?
Interpreting user needs means figuring out what the end-user actually needs to accomplish instead of just guessing what features to build. Creating user stories helps with this because it frames requirements directly from the user's perspective. By breaking down the work into clear scenarios, it keeps the focus on why a feature is needed and makes large projects much easier to organize into actionable steps.

How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?
I approach development by working iteratively—building out core functionality first and then adding features step-by-step rather than trying to write everything all at once. In future projects, I want to keep using Agile practices like sprint planning to manage the workload and regular retrospectives to review what went well and where we can improve.

What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?
Being a good team member means being reliable, communicating clearly, and supporting your peers. It involves doing your share of the work on time, letting the team know early if you hit a roadblock, and giving helpful feedback during code reviews. Ultimately, it is about keeping the project moving forward as a team rather than working in isolation.
